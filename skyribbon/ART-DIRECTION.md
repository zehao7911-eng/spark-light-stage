# A little island fair, built from ribbons

Deep navy #182e4b anchors the interface; coral #ef987d, sea glass #85bcb5, lavender #b19dc9 and warm ticket gold carry the foreground. Islands and distant palms use quieter greens. Each park changes sky, sea and accent together, retaining the same material and shape families. A ticket icon denotes spendable currency; glints and best-rating stars have separate labels.

Track width is fixed. Brass rails sit under the wheel centres, pale sleepers connect them, and slender supports end below the track. Car bodies are 2.35 units long with 3.2-unit arc spacing; all five cars fit on the extended, level station rails. Seats, passenger legs, seat backs and lap bars have explicit positions. The canopy is above the highest rider, outside the rail clearance. Tall decorative attractions stay behind the playable corridor; low foreground boats stay below the lowest track.

The camera follows the whole train, not just its leading car. Car corners are checked against the viewport; it widens for longer trains or changes of grade. A forward three-quarter angle shows passengers and the wheel contact. Stationary drawings still have a labelled moving preview, while only a real ride awards glints. The small plot shows the full hill profile; the main view can stay close enough to make the train readable on a phone.

Water uses broad undulation, soft irregular highlights and distant haze, with foam around low-poly cliffs. Ferris cabins counter-rotate to stay upright. Boats bob subtly. These are secondary motions; the coral carriages, warm glints and rail line remain the readable focal shapes. Night scenes use the same geometry and warm lanterns rather than switching to an unrelated art style.

Collected glints emit a short star burst above the car body. Every third glint adds a small celebratory bloom; boost adds a sea-glass burst and changes the charge/button state. Effects are bounded and pause with the ride. New parks should extend this kit with a distinct landmark or lighting treatment before adding detail density. Do not replace the actual ride with a painted mockup or mix incompatible character scales.
