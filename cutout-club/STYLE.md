# A little look, a little world

## Construction

Editorial paper cutouts, thin charcoal contours and short fabric hatching. The original figure is slender and fully dressed, with a small human face, drawn hair strands, a cut-paper edge and planted shoes. Thin white borders show the stacking order; they belong to paper silhouettes rather than every facial detail. Faces have restrained eyelids, a fine nose and a small asymmetric smile. Clothing follows common shoulder, waist, hand and ankle coordinates across Jun, Sol and Nori.

The logical stage is 420×600. Main contours are about 1.1 px; internal pencil marks 0.5–0.8 px; the cream paper edge is about 4.5 px. A roughly 400-unit-tall figure fills the paper arch. Feet rest at y486 on the y487 pedestal. Arms are behind sleeves; hands remain visible for carried props. Hair sits behind the face and hats. Carry items sit in front after the body and head are assembled. The umbrella canopy stays to the right of the face. Winter outerwear includes neutral tights under the coat.

## Materials and scene

Use grouped short pencil strokes for woven cloth, regular lines for sailor stripes, curves for puffer quilting, narrow creases for pleats and a separate ribbon silhouette for tulle. Garments have their own authored silhouettes, not just recoloured rectangles. Small buttons, seams, straps, buckles, folded collars and embroidered marks identify function. Paper grain is a cached deterministic field of faint fibres; it does not replace the main outlines or fabric structures.

The window/arch and stage registration stay constant across ten briefs. Rain, sun, snow and night change the palette and sparse distant scenery. Buildings and window divisions have less contrast than the figure. Tape and pencil annotations sit near the perimeter. Foreground plant leaves, the pedestal, item thumbnails, interface icons and saved postcards share the same drawn language. Do not mix high-resolution photography, pixel sprites or glossy 3D into this system.

## Feedback and interface

Selecting an item lifts and settles that paper layer in a half-second motion, with a short fabric-like synthesized cue and a few paper scraps. Matching wishes use a tick, text and colour, so colour alone is not required. The shutter accepts the exact current outfit, then reveals a framed postcard and newly earned item samples. The postcard uses the real renderer and saved IDs. Sparse serif headlines, tiny sans-serif captions, thin rules and cream/red buttons match editorial stationery. Primary hit areas remain at least 44 CSS px.

`artbook.html` shows the palette, three full-scene samples and all forty original item thumbnails. These are labelled art fixtures, not earned game progress or exported reference artwork. Extend by adding a registered garment path, its structural hatching, thumbnail bounds and catalogue metadata, then verifying it on all three figure treatments.
